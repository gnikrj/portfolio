# Jeff King — Data & Analytics Portfolio

I work in finance and order-to-cash, and separately spend a lot of time on
voting rights and democracy work in Durham, NC (including with **Bull City
Votes**). This site is where those two threads meet: I take the same
analytical habits I use to prioritize collections work — scoring, ranking,
gap analysis — and point them at public civic data.

## Projects

### 🗳️ [Durham County Voter Registration & Turnout Analysis](https://github.com/gnikrj/portfolio/tree/main/projects/durham-voter-turnout-analysis)
Where does Durham County's registration and turnout actually lag, and for
whom? Combines NC SBE voter registration and history files with 2020 Census
population data at the precinct level to find the gaps — and what's
correlated with them.

### 📊 [A Prioritization Model for Voter Outreach](https://github.com/gnikrj/portfolio/tree/main/projects/voter-outreach-prioritization)
**Flagship piece.** Takes the same account-prioritization scoring logic
used in order-to-cash collections and applies it to the turnout-analysis
findings above: which Durham precincts should a registration/turnout
campaign prioritize first, and why.

### 💵 [AR Aging & Collections Prioritizer](https://github.com/gnikrj/portfolio/tree/main/projects/ar-collections-prioritizer)
A synthetic order-to-cash dataset — DSO, aging buckets, and a scoring model
for which accounts to chase first. The finance-side counterpart to the
prioritization model above.

### 🧩 [Bending Spoons Acquisition-Integration Model](https://github.com/gnikrj/portfolio/tree/main/projects/bending-spoons-integration-model)
Bending Spoons grows by acquisition, so integration speed is a finance
question. Using its Q2 2026 release, deal announcements and the acquired
companies' old 10-Ks, this Excel model walks leverage through Airtable and
Miro against the covenant, shows that each $100M of documented savings is
worth roughly $400M of acquisition capacity, prices a month of Miro
integration delay at ~$29M, and ends with the integration scorecard and
12-month look-back I'd run on every deal.
